An honest AirConsole alternative

AirConsole and Steer It work the same way — one screen, everyone's phone as the controller, nothing to download. But they are different kinds of product, and which one suits you depends on what you actually want from a games night. Here's the straight version, weaknesses and all.

What AirConsole is great at

AirConsole is a library. It offers 150+ games on desktop and 60+ on Android TV, spread across genres — trivia, party classics, shooters, drawing games, co-op puzzles and more. It's an established product with a large user base and a real track record, and it runs on a lot of screens: computer, Android TV, Amazon Fire TV and tablets.

If what you want is variety — a different mini-game every evening, something for every kind of group — AirConsole is the better choice, and we'll say so plainly. Its free "Starter Pack" gives you a weekly rotating selection, but it's capped at a maximum of two players and comes with ad breaks. To open up the full library you take the AirConsole Hero subscription (auto-renewing monthly; a non-renewing annual pass also exists).

What Steer It is

Steer It is the opposite bet: one game, done properly. It's a racing party game where each phone becomes a tilt steering wheel and up to eight cars share a single screen. Under the arcade surface is a genuine per-wheel physics model, so the driving has real feel — you flick the car into a slide, catch it with a bit of countersteer, and power out. There's Time Attack with online leaderboards, and ghost racing where you chase a translucent replay of your own best lap.

It runs in any modern browser. Host it on a laptop, desktop or tablet — any device with a capable browser, including smart TVs whose browser can handle it; players join by scanning a QR code with their phones. Nothing to install on the screen or on anyone's phone.

Where Steer It is genuinely the better pick

There are three honest reasons to choose Steer It over AirConsole:

  • Depth on one axis. If racing is the thing you want, a specialist beats a sampler. One well-built driving model with drifting, lap times and ghosts gives you more to come back to than a racing mini-game inside a big collection.
  • A free tier that doesn't stop at two. Steer It's free Free Ride mode supports up to eight players on a choice of arcade cars across three maps (Circuit, Desktop and the Stadium Oval), with no account required to start driving.
  • Pay once, not forever. Everything else — every map and mode, the simulation cars and no ads — is a single $6.90 purchase. No subscription, no auto-renew.

Where AirConsole wins

A comparison that only flatters one side isn't worth reading, so here's the other column plainly. AirConsole has far more content and genres than a single racing game ever will. It has a longer track record and a bigger community. And it runs natively on more living-room platforms — Android TV and Fire TV apps, not just a browser tab. If breadth matters more to you than depth, or your group's taste is all over the place, AirConsole is the sensible call.

So which should you pick?

If you want a games collection for every mood, go with AirConsole — that's what it's for. If you want one racing game with real depth, a free tier that fits the whole room, and no subscription hanging over it, that's Steer It. It's free to try in your browser right now — no download, no account to start.
