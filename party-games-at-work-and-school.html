Party games for the office and the classroom

The best game for a work break or a gap between lessons is the one nobody has to set up. No installs, no controllers to dig out, no "can everyone download the app first". Steer It is a browser racing party game where each person's phone becomes the wheel — which is exactly what a room full of laptops and phones is already equipped for. Seriously, it's great for breaks. And yes, for that boring lecture too — but we didn't say that.

Why it works at work and at school

Offices and schools are the two places where "just install this" goes to die. Locked-down laptops, no admin rights, an app store you can't reach, and an IT ticket for anything that isn't already on the approved list. A browser game steps around all of it: there's nothing to install, so usually no admin rights or IT request to run it, and nothing to fetch from an app store. It runs on whatever screen is already in the room — a meeting-room display, the computer that drives a projector, or just a laptop on the table — and there is nothing to book. You open a web page; that's the whole deployment.

Setup takes seconds

One person opens the game on the shared screen and it shows a QR code. Everyone else points a phone camera at it and they're in — that phone is now a steering wheel. No accounts to create just to play, no pairing, no cables. Getting from "shall we?" to a full grid of cars takes about as long as it takes to say it.

It fits an actual break

This is the part that matters at work and school: a session is exactly as long as you have. Jump into Free Ride for five minutes and drop out when the break's over — there is no fixed round you're obliged to finish, so nobody is stranded mid-game when it's time to get back to it. Up to eight people can play on one screen at once, and it's just as good with two — enough for the whole team, or just whoever happens to be at their desk.

What you need

  • One screen with a web browser to host — a laptop or desktop, the computer that drives a meeting-room display or projector, or a smart TV with a capable browser.
  • A phone for each player, with a camera to scan the QR code.
  • The same Wi‑Fi is smoothest, which is the normal case when you're all in one room.

That's the whole kit. No console, no controllers, no downloads — nothing that needs signing off first.

For teams and managers

It's the low-effort version of the things that are meant to be fun but usually arrive as a calendar invite. A ten-minute reset before a long meeting, a lunchtime race, or a team social that doesn't need anyone to book a room or expense a thing. Because it starts in seconds and runs on the screen you already have, it's the kind of thing you can suggest on the spot instead of planning it a week out — and it's yours to start, out in the open, not something anyone has to run past you.

For teachers

Hosting on a projector or a classroom board works the same way: open Steer It on the computer that drives the screen, and every student joins from their own phone by scanning the QR code. No accounts are needed to play, so there's no sign-up wall between "let's try this" and the whole class driving. It's a low-friction way to spend the last few minutes of a lesson or reward a group — something you run yourself, on the big screen, with the whole room in on it.
